**One-line summary**: As open-source AI moves into the mainstream, Hugging Face leaders spoke to TechCrunch and Wired about who captures the value, the trade-offs of building on open models, and where they believe AI risk really originates.

### Key points
- TechCrunch reports that enterprises, governments and startups are relying more on open-source models, drawn by lower costs, more control and more flexibility.
- As more capable models become freely available, a central question is how companies capture value.
- Chinese open models are gaining ground, raising the question of whether American players can keep up.
- Hugging Face's policy chief discussed the costs, risks and trade-offs of building on open-source AI.
- According to Wired, CEO Clément Delangue put his P(doom) "close to zero" and argued that most risk is created by a few players building behind closed doors.

### What happened
TechCrunch published the same discussion in two formats, a video and a podcast episode. The video's headline emphasizes the money question: who profits from the open-source AI boom. The podcast headline instead centers on Hugging Face's policy chief and the costs, risks and trade-offs facing anyone who builds on open models. Both share the same description: open-source models are becoming more mainstream because of the promise of lower costs, greater control and flexibility.

That description poses two open questions. First, if increasingly capable models are freely available, how do companies capture value? Second, with Chinese open models gaining ground, can American players keep pace? The material available from TechCrunch frames these questions but does not, in its description, provide specific answers or figures.

Wired covered a different angle from what appears to be a Wired World Fair 2026 appearance. Delangue said his P(doom), the estimated probability of AI-driven catastrophe, is close to zero. His case for open-source AI rests on the claim that most of the risk is created by a couple of players developing technology behind closed doors. Where TechCrunch focused on markets and policy, Wired highlighted the safety argument for openness.

### Why it matters
Taken together, the three pieces show the open-source AI debate widening from model performance to business models, international competition and safety philosophy. The wider models circulate, the harder it may be to earn money from a model alone, which is why the question of where value accrues has become pressing. The rise of Chinese open models adds a geopolitical layer to that discussion.

Readers should also note the speakers' position: Hugging Face is a company whose platform is built around open models, so advocacy for openness aligns with its own business direction. The sources here do not describe concrete consequences for specific regions or users, so none are asserted.
