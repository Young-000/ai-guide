**One-line summary**: A biotech startup let an AI agent pick a gene therapy, then had it inject the treatment into a live mouse's brain, and the injection succeeded.

### Key points
- The agent itself chose which gene therapy to use, rather than a human researcher.
- It carried the work through to injecting the chosen treatment into the brain of a living mouse, which was reported as successful.
- The test subject was a live animal, so this goes beyond research AI that stays with literature search or data analysis and moves into physical experiments.
- Wired framed the result as a startling glimpse of what future research labs could look like.

### Why it matters
If AI moves from proposing hypotheses to selecting treatments and carrying out experiments, research speed and staffing models could change significantly. At the same time, handing in-vivo work to an agent raises questions about safety validation, accountability, and oversight that need to be addressed alongside the technology.
