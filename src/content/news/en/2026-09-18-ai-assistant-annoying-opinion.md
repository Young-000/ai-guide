**In one line**: A Washington Post column argues that AI assistants' constant intrusions are annoying — but the deeper issue is trust and control.

### Key points
- The author describes everyday AI assistants as "deeply annoying" in practice.
- The central argument, however, is that the irritation masks a larger concern — intervention without clear consent, delegated judgment, and growing dependence.
- As an opinion piece, it appears to critique how AI assistants seep into daily life broadly, rather than targeting a single product.

### Why it matters
As Big Tech makes AI assistants the default across operating systems, search, and productivity tools, this is a voice flagging the shift in control behind the "convenience." Designing for user trust is becoming as decisive as the feature race itself.
