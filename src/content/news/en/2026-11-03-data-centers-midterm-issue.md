**In one line**: With the US midterms arriving, AI and data centers surfaced as a shared topic both in conversations at a Brooklyn political gathering and in an interview with New York City's mayor.

### Key points
- TechCrunch described a midterm party in Brooklyn held five days before Election Day, where talk ranging from a council member's fireside chat to a debate about the Democratic Party kept circling back to AI and data centers.
- WIRED interviewed NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani ahead of the midterms on data centers, AI in schools and the future of the Democratic Party.
- The WIRED interview is titled "Our Risk, Their Reward," a phrase that suggests a framing of data centers as a question of who bears the risks and who collects the benefits.
- Both pieces treat AI infrastructure as a matter of local politics and party direction, not only a tech-industry story.

### What happened
TechCrunch offered a scene report from a midterm party in Brooklyn, five days before Election Day. According to the outlet, the subject of conversation varied, yet it repeatedly converged on AI and data centers. That held whether the setting was a council member's fireside chat or a debate over where the Democratic Party is heading. The headline's "even in Brooklyn" signals the writer's point: the topic has reached places not usually associated with infrastructure debates.

WIRED took a different route, speaking directly with a public official. Its interview with Mayor Mamdani covered data centers, the use of AI in schools, and the Democratic Party's future. The title, "Our Risk, Their Reward," reads as shorthand for a concern that communities absorb the downsides while the gains go elsewhere. That is the framing the title conveys; the mayor's specific remarks are in the interview itself.

The two pieces differ in vantage point. TechCrunch focused on how often political participants raise the subject in everyday talk, a view from the ground up. WIRED focused on how a city leader defines the subject, a view from the top down. Three elements appear in both: data centers, AI, and the identity of the Democratic Party.

### Why it matters
Data centers have mostly been discussed in terms of corporate investment and power demand. These two reports show the same subject becoming a central topic in election-season conversation and in a mayoral interview. That suggests AI infrastructure is increasingly seen as an issue that touches residents' lives and shapes party positioning.

The pairing of data centers with AI in schools in the same interview is also notable. It implies that a physical facility and a classroom technology question are being discussed within one political frame. Neither report addresses effects on readers outside the United States, so this piece does not speculate on them, but the pattern of data center siting and AI policy turning into a local political issue is worth watching.
