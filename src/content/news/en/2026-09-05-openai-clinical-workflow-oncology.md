**In one line**: OpenAI's AI is reportedly entering hospital clinical workflows, and oncology is being singled out as the area that could best prove its real-world value.

### Key points
- In an Oncodaily piece, Jordan Johnson examines OpenAI's move into clinical medical settings.
- The framing is that AI is entering the **clinical workflow itself**, not just peripheral tasks like note-taking or triage.
- **Oncology** — with its dense data and complex decisions — is presented as the field most likely to surface AI's genuine value.

### Why it matters
Medicine adopts AI slowly because errors are costly and regulation is strict. Delivering results in a hard domain like oncology could push AI from an "assistive tool" toward a recognized partner in clinical decision-making. Safety, validation, and accountability, however, remain open questions.
