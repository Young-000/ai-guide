**In one line**: A US federal appeals court reportedly upheld the Pentagon's supply chain risk designation, leaving it in force after judicial review.

### Key points
- According to MeriTalk, the US Court of Appeals for the DC Circuit rejected a legal challenge and upheld the Pentagon's "supply chain risk" designation.
- The ruling appears to leave the procurement-related designation intact after clearing judicial scrutiny.
- The court's full reasoning and the designation's exact scope should be confirmed against the original report and the opinion itself.

### Why it matters
In government procurement, a supply chain risk designation is a powerful tool that can effectively limit a supplier's ability to win contracts. By upholding it on appeal, the court reaffirms the discretion agencies hold in security vetting of AI and technology procurement — and the limits of judicial review over it.
