**In one line**: Drugmakers appear to have stretched their exclusivity on individual drugs by layering on secondary patents.

### Key points
- Stacking add-on patents reportedly extended drug monopoly periods from roughly two years to more than six.
- The "patent thicket" strategy adds surrounding patents — on formulation, dosing, and the like — after the original patent expires, delaying competitors.
- Longer exclusivity pushes back generic entry, keeping prices elevated.

### Why it matters
Critics argue patent stacking rewards monopoly defense rather than genuine innovation. The longer exclusivity runs, the longer consumers and insurers shoulder higher prices — keeping this at the center of patent-reform debates.
