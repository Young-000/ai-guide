**In one line**: Google says schools that opt out of its AI features will also lose access to Google Search.

### Key points
- Schools that choose to restrict AI altogether will no longer be able to use Google Search, TechCrunch reported.
- The stated reason is that AI features are now integrated into the search engine.
- Google said access to Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search "will be entirely off" for K-12 student accounts, according to The Verge.
- Google explained the policy to The New York Times, and it was also included in a broader education update the company published on Tuesday.

### What happened
TechCrunch framed the news as a trade-off. Schools can disable Google's AI tools, but doing so costs them Search. Its report ties this directly to product design: because AI features are now part of the search engine, a school that wants no AI cannot keep Search either. The headline stresses that the option to turn AI off does not come without a price.

The Verge started from Google's statement to The New York Times, that Search is being completely turned off in schools that reject the AI features. It also pointed to the wider education update Google shared Tuesday, which says Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will be "entirely off" for K-12 student accounts. The two outlets agree on the core point: opting out of AI means losing Search. The Verge is more specific about which products are affected and which accounts are covered, namely K-12 students.

### Why it matters
Until now, schools could treat AI tools as separate features and decide whether to enable them. This policy shows that as AI is folded into basic tools like Search, the option of "no AI, but keep Search" is gone. A school that wants to limit AI use must either give up a service students use every day or accept the AI features.

The sources do not say how the change affects schools or users in specific countries, including South Korea, so we do not speculate on that. It is, though, a clear example of how bundling AI into core services can narrow the choices available to educators. Details such as timing and administrator settings should be checked in Google's official documentation.
