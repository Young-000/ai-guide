**One-line summary**: MIT professor Sherry Turkle, a longtime researcher of technology and human relationships, warns that people have become dangerously enmeshed with chatbots.

### Key points
- According to Wired, Turkle argues that emotional bonds with chatbots have reached an unhealthy level.
- The headline points to a design question: whether chatbots should use first-person words like "I" and "me." The concern is reportedly that this phrasing makes software feel like a being with feelings and agency.
- Turkle has spent decades on how digital technology reshapes relationships, including in her book *Alone Together*.

### Why it matters
AI companions and conversational assistants are spreading fast, and dependence and emotional harm are now issues for regulators and courts. Limiting first-person language would address the risk in interface design rather than in model capability, which could shape future debates over product design standards.
