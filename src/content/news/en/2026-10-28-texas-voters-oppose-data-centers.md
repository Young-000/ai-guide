**One-line summary**: Across the Austin area in Texas, plans for new data centers keep running into resistance from local residents.

### Key points
- Gary Bledsoe, president of the Texas NAACP, has watched data center developers descend on Austin and its surrounding suburbs.
- Bledsoe says he can list project after project that is facing opposition from locals.
- One example is Taylor, where residents are fighting a plan to build a data center on a site that was supposed to be a park.
- The Verge reports that Texas voters are in agreement on the issue.

### Why it matters
As AI demand drives rapid data center growth, where to put them is turning into a local political fight. Sites that touch residents' daily lives, such as land earmarked for a park, can draw stronger pushback, adding permitting and timeline uncertainty for developers.
