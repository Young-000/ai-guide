**One-line summary**: Eiliko, an emotion-reactive AI pet robot, reportedly keeps user conversations on Chinese servers, and that stored data may be exposed to attackers.

### Key Points
- Eiliko is a small, marshmallow-shaped digital pet that switches between cartoon expressions to mirror the user's mood.
- When used over Wi-Fi, the device reportedly connects to servers in China, and its AI companion feature can store nearly everything users say to it.
- The Verge reports that this stored data is at risk of being hacked. The article URL suggests the findings come from a security group called Energize Lab.
- Because the device is marketed as a toy, children may be among its main users, which raises the sensitivity of the conversations it collects.

### Why It Matters
For AI companion devices, the conversations are the core data, so server security and storage location translate directly into privacy risk. As low-cost AI toys spread, buyers have more reason to check data retention policies and whether a device works offline before buying.
