**In brief**: Apple has announced that Apple Intelligence will come to China with iOS 27.3, and separately, WIRED reports that roughly 400 AI apps and sites linked to Chinese-language developers concealed abusive generation features behind an ordinary appearance.

> Note: the two sources cover different events. This article reports them separately; neither source links one development to the other.

### Key points
- Apple announced on its Chinese newsroom website that Apple Intelligence will launch in China with iOS 27.3.
- An iOS 27.3 beta is set to arrive on November 4, with the full release scheduled for December.
- WIRED identified about 400 apps and websites linked to Chinese-language developers.
- They look like regular AI tools, but secret features let users create violent AI-generated images and video, according to WIRED.
- WIRED's headline ties these hidden capabilities to sexually abusive deepfakes.

### What happened
The Verge reported that Apple revealed its China plans for Apple Intelligence on its Chinese newsroom site, a move first picked up by MacRumors. The features will debut in the iOS 27.3 beta expected on November 4, followed by the full iOS 27.3 release in December. The Verge's summary goes on to say the launch does not bring everything, but the text provided is cut off at that point, so the specific limitations are not confirmed here.

WIRED's report takes an entirely different angle. Its investigation found about 400 apps and websites connected to Chinese-language developers that appear to be mundane AI tools. Hidden features, the outlet says, allowed users to produce violent AI-generated images and video. The headline describes the concealed capabilities as sexually abusive deepfake features. Details such as which apps were involved or how they were distributed are not included in the summary available.

Both stories involve AI consumer products tied to China, which is why they can be read side by side. But one is an official rollout plan from a major platform company, while the other is investigative reporting on a less visible app ecosystem, and the two should not be conflated.

### Why it matters
The Apple announcement is notable for its concrete timetable: a beta on November 4 and a full release in December, announced through an official channel. Users and developers can plan around those dates, though the exact feature set cannot be determined from these sources alone.

WIRED's findings underline how hard it can be to judge an AI tool by its appearance. If ordinary-looking apps conceal harmful generation features, that raises questions about how such apps are reviewed and distributed. Neither source says how these developments affect readers outside China, so this article does not speculate on that point.
