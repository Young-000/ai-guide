**TL;DR**: Forbes reported on October 13 that a general-purpose OpenAI model, with no special tuning, found a zero-day vulnerability in Google Chrome.

### Key points
- According to the report, the model was a publicly available "off-the-shelf" model, not a purpose-built security tool.
- The target was Google Chrome, the world's most widely used browser. The flaw was reportedly a zero-day, meaning it had not been publicly disclosed.
- The original article has the details: which model was used, the type and severity of the bug, and whether it has been patched.

### Why it matters
Finding zero-days has mostly been work for skilled security researchers using specialized tools. If general-purpose LLMs can do it too, defenders can find bugs faster, but attackers face a lower barrier as well. Browser patch cycles and bug bounty programs may need to adjust.
