**In one line**: Tech column spyglass.org asks whether anyone can finally architect a real personal assistant AI, spotlighting the limits of today's assistants.

### Key points
- The piece appears to argue that while conversational chatbots are everywhere, a genuine personal assistant — one that spans a user's context, calendar, devices, and data — is still missing.
- It reads as commentary on why long-running voice assistants (Siri, Alexa, and the like) remain unfinished even in the LLM era.
- Notably, it frames the challenge less as a model-capability problem and more as an "architecture" problem: data access, platform integration, and trust.

### Why it matters
A personal assistant AI is the next prize every big-tech player is chasing, but the argument is that raw model performance alone won't crack it. Who integrates a user's data — and how much they can be trusted to act on the user's behalf — is presented as the real battleground.
