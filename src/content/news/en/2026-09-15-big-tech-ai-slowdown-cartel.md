**In one line**: Leading AI figures loosely agreed to slow development, prompting immediate suspicion that "safety" masks an effort to limit competition.

### Key points
- Over the weekend, Sam Altman (OpenAI), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind cofounder), and Elon Musk (SpaceX) reportedly agreed loosely to slow AI development.
- They framed the goal as an effort to "pace the frontier."
- Skeptics quickly argued the safety-branded agreement could function as a cartel that restrains competition.

### Why it matters
When a handful of frontier firms jointly agree to slow down, the move can be read two ways at once: as responsible safety coordination, or as anti-competitive behavior that keeps rivals and new entrants out. That distinction is likely to sit at the center of coming AI regulation and antitrust debates.
