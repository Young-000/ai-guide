**One-line summary**: Meta has added "Spaces" to its Muse AI agent, allowing the assistant to build customized, app-like screens that users can work with directly inside the Muse app.

### Key points
- The new feature is called Spaces and creates customized, app-like experiences within Muse.
- Examples named include a shopping hub, a school dashboard, a music tracker and a trivia game.
- Users interact with what the assistant builds directly in the Muse app.
- TechCrunch places Spaces within a steady stream of new features and integrations for Meta's Muse agents.
- Both outlets reported the news on November 6.

### What happened
TechCrunch framed the announcement as "a new way to collaborate with your Muse agent." Its account notes that Meta's Muse agents keep gaining features and integrations, and says Spaces promises a new way to interact with the AI helpers. The emphasis is on working alongside the agent.

The Verge described the feature in more concrete terms. According to its report, Meta will now let people build customized, app-like experiences within Muse, and the feature is called "spaces." The AI assistant can build things such as a shopping hub, school dashboard, music tracker or trivia game, which a user can then use directly in the Muse app. The headline stresses that the assistant can produce the version of an app you need right now.

Read together, the two pieces describe the same feature from different angles. TechCrunch focuses on the collaborative relationship with an agent, while The Verge focuses on the app-like output that gets generated on demand. The Verge's summary also begins to explain how a user creates a new space, but the passage is cut off, so the exact steps are not covered here.

### Why it matters
AI assistants have largely been conversational tools: you ask, and they answer in text. Spaces points toward a different role, where the result of a request is something you can reopen and use repeatedly, such as a dashboard, tracker or game. The examples given, covering shopping, school, music and play, are everyday uses rather than specialist ones, which suggests the feature is aimed at general users.

The idea also fits a broader pattern in which a single AI app absorbs tasks that might otherwise require separate apps, since the tools are created inside Muse as needed. Neither source says when or where Spaces will be available, including in Korea, or under what conditions, so those details remain unconfirmed. Further announcements from Meta should clarify how widely the feature will roll out.
