**Summary**: Apple has begun beta testing its new Audio Intelligence features for Apple Watch through the watchOS 27.3 developer beta, including Siri Recap for conversation summaries and Live Rewind for recent-conversation transcripts.

### Key points
- The new feature set is called Audio Intelligence and is rolling out in the watchOS 27.3 developer beta.
- Two features are included: Siri Recap, which uses "ambient listening" to generate summaries of conversations, and Live Rewind, which produces text transcripts of what was just said.
- The features let Siri tune into the Apple Watch's microphone. The Verge describes them as "always-on."
- TechCrunch frames the purpose as helping users remember what someone said or take notes on conversations.

### What happened
TechCrunch reported that Apple's new Audio Intelligence features have entered beta testing. Its emphasis is on the practical benefit: helping people remember what another person said, or capturing notes from a conversation. The headline highlights two actions, rewinding and recapping.

The Verge covered the same rollout with more detail on how the features are built. It said that, starting today, Siri can tune into the Apple Watch's mic, and that the capability ships in the watchOS 27.3 developer beta now rolling out. It named the two additions: Siri Recap, which relies on ambient listening to generate summaries of a conversation, and Live Rewind, which generates text transcripts of the conversation that just took place. The outlet grouped them under "always-on" Audio Intelligence.

The two accounts are consistent and complement each other. TechCrunch focuses on what users get out of the feature, while The Verge supplies the feature names, the mechanism (ambient listening and text transcripts) and the distribution channel (the watchOS 27.3 developer beta). Neither summary establishes a public release date, which devices or languages are supported, or how recorded audio and transcripts are stored and processed.

### Why it matters
Siri on the watch has traditionally responded when called. Features that listen ambiently and then summarize or transcribe represent a different model, in which the device captures context and organizes it for the wearer. Because the watch is worn all day, such tools could be used in meetings or everyday conversations without opening a separate app.

An always-listening microphone also raises questions about how other people's speech is handled, and those questions are likely to draw attention as the feature moves toward wider release. For now, what is confirmed is limited to the developer beta. Whether the features stay unchanged in the final release, and when they reach users in other regions such as South Korea, is not stated in the sources and will depend on what Apple publishes after the beta period.
