**In one line**: Robots could technically handle most US physical labor, but an analysis finds only a tiny fraction of those tasks are actually cheaper to automate than to staff with people.

### Key points
- An Anthropic analysis reportedly estimates robots are **technically capable** of roughly 74% of US physical work tasks.
- Yet robots are **cheaper than human labor** in only 0.3% of those tasks — a stark gap between capability and economics.
- The figures underline that "a robot can do it" and "a robot can do it more cheaply than a person" are very different questions.

### Why it matters
Automation anxiety often fixates on what robots are capable of, but real-world displacement is driven by **unit cost**. As long as hardware and operating costs exceed wages, technical feasibility alone won't translate into job replacement — a case for measured expectations.
