**In one line**: India is requiring caller-ID apps such as Truecaller to feed their user spam reports to telecom operators.

### Key points
- India's regulator mandates that caller-ID apps share spam-report data with telcos on a **one-way basis**.
- Truecaller objects, saying the rule hands a "commercially valuable proprietary asset" to operators.
- The data is built from user-submitted reports and underpins the apps' spam-detection models.

### Why it matters
Spam- and fraud-call detection is a textbook case of AI systems trained on crowdsourced user reports. Forcing that data to be shared strips away a data-built competitive edge and reopens questions about privacy handling — a tension that could spread to other markets.
