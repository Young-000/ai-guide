**In one line**: BBC's nature series Blue Planet III is keeping less-than-perfect shots in the final cut to show that its footage is genuine.

### Key points
- Earlier entries in the series were known for gorgeous, crystal-clear footage that seemed almost too good to be real.
- This time the team is leaving imperfect shots in, as a way to prove authenticity, according to The Verge.
- The backdrop is a tell of AI-generated video: images that look too polished and seamless are a giveaway.
- What has been reported concerns the production approach. Which specific shots were kept is not covered in the article summary.

### Why it matters
Visual perfection used to signal craftsmanship. Now an overly clean picture can invite suspicion that it was synthesized. For a genre like nature documentary, where trust rests on real-world filming, leaving rough texture intact can work as proof of provenance. It is an example of how the standard for "polish" in video is shifting.
