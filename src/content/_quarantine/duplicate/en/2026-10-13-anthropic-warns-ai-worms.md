**In one line**: Anthropic has reportedly warned that "AI worms" could move from one device to the next with no human involved.

### Key points
- Cryptonews.net reports that Anthropic sees a risk of AI-driven malware spreading across devices on its own, much like a virus.
- The main worry is autonomy. An attacker would not need to steer each step, because the AI itself could find its next target and copy itself there.
- The headline does not say how these worms would spread, whether any have been seen in the wild, or what defenses Anthropic suggests. Those details are in the original report.
- The idea has been shown in research before. In 2024, researchers demonstrated "Morris II," a worm that used prompt injection to jump between generative-AI email assistants.

### Why it matters
AI agents are getting more access to email, files, code execution and outside tools. If one agent is compromised, the infection could reach every agent and device it is connected to. When a company that builds these models raises the alarm itself, it shows that limiting agent permissions and checking their inputs are becoming central security problems.
